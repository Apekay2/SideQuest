Re-engineering ride-hailing for errands

Side Qwest borrows ride-hailing's spine — verified supply, geospatial matching, escrowed money, a live job state machine — and changes the three things that make errands a different business: the job is a shopping mandate, not a ride, the price is bid, not metered, and the platform, not the runner, pays the merchant.

Spend authority is not a wallet debit but a one-time virtual card issued per errand, loaded in tranches as proof arrives and voided when the errand closes. What counts as proof depends on the errand type. Escrow sits behind the card and protects both sides: while money is frozen, neither party can be stranded, and contested cases are decided by Legal Operations on the evidence the errand already collected. This document specifies the services, data model, matching logic and payment rails for the Nairobi pilot. Money is KSh, settlement runs over M-Pesa (Daraja), and identity is gated: an unverified account can browse and nothing else.

4. Matching and the bid auction

Matching proposes; the requester decides. On errand.posted, matching scores available runners inside a 5 km radius and notifies the top 25. The auction service opens a 12-minute window; bids are sealed — a runner sees the guide range and nothing else, which stops the price race that open bidding produces in thin markets.

score = 0.35 · proximity (haversine, decayed over 5 km) + 0.25 · reliability (completion rate, last 90 days) + 0.20 · category_fit (past errands of this type) + 0.10 · rating (Bayesian, prior = platform mean) + 0.10 · freshness (inverse of jobs awarded today — spreads work) − penalties (open dispute, expiring KYC, cancel rate > 8%)

Figure 2 — shortlist ranking. Weights are config, not code; the pilot tunes them weekly.

Window mechanics: first bid starts a 12-minute countdown, extended to 4 minutes remaining whenever a new bid lands, capped at 25 minutes. The requester may award at any point. On award, errand moves to awarded, losing bids are released with a reason, and the shortlist is discarded. On expiry with no award, the errand re-posts once at a widened radius with the guide range nudged up by the median unawarded bid.

Errand states: draft → posted → bidding → awarded → en_route → at_source → basket_pending → basket_approved → in_transit → handover_pending → completed, with cancelled and disputed reachable from most states. Transitions are the only writers of the errand row and each emits an event.

Deadlines. A requester may put a clock on an errand — a deadline_at and an optional on-time bonus funded from their wallet alongside the fee. The deadline is a matching input (runners who cannot make the window are not shortlisted), a display on every bid card, and a settlement input: the bonus pays only if handover.scanned_at precedes the deadline. A missed deadline is not a breach and never forfeits the fee; it feeds the reliability term in the shortlist score.

Two paths that skip the auction. A relationship row is written each time a pair completes an errand together; past requesters may then invite that runner directly, which creates an errand already in awarded at a proposed fee, with a decline returning it to open bidding. Separately, runners at tier 3 with a rating and completion rate above threshold may batch: accept several errands that share a region_id and run them on one trip. Batching is trip planning, not money pooling — each errand in a batch keeps its own card, its own approvals, its own escrow and its own dispute surface, and the matcher refuses a batch spanning regions rather than optimising across them.

5. Money movement: the one-time Qwest card

Two constraints shape the design. The runner never spends their own money, and the requester never pays for goods nobody can evidence. A per-errand virtual card satisfies both: it is the only instrument the runner can spend, it holds nothing until proof arrives, and it stops existing when the errand does.

  1. Fund escrow at award. An STK push moves spend_cap = goods ceiling + agreed fee + 15% headroom into the platform escrow account. This is the only time the requester is debited.
  2. Issue the card. The card service mints a single-use card against that escrow balance: zero initial load, MCC allowlist for the errand type, geo-fenced where the issuer supports it, hard expiry at errand close + 30 minutes. The runner sees it only inside the app.
  3. Load in tranches. Each accepted proof event loads exactly one tranche — never the whole cap. A load is a ledger posting from escrow to card float, not a new charge to the requester.
  4. Settle and void. On completion the card is voided, unspent float returns to escrow, the runner's fee less commission is paid out to M-Pesa, and the requester is refunded the difference between cap and actual spend.
  5. Unhappy paths. Expiry, cancellation, a failed proof or an opened report voids the card immediately and freezes the escrow balance in place. The ledger records reversals; nothing is edited.

What counts as proof — and therefore who is asked to act — is a property of the errand type, carried on the basket as release_mode:

Vendor legs are what make market runs work: one basket, several legs, each moving through shopping → pending → approved → paid on its own. Evidence gates the send, the requester gates the load. A leg can be sent for approval only when every item on it carries a photo whose vision score clears threshold and whose price sits inside the market band; the requester then approves that leg and its subtotal — and only its subtotal — loads onto the card. Item photos are also the evidence pack a dispute later runs on, which is why they are captured at purchase rather than reconstructed afterwards.

Two exception paths interrupt that flow. A photo below threshold is rejected for retake; a second failure escalates the line to the requester, who may approve it on their own judgement — the override is recorded on the item and carried into the evidence pack. An item that is unavailable becomes a substitution proposal: the runner names the replacement and its price, and the requester accepts it (the line is repriced) or drops it (the line is refunded to zero). Neither path lets the runner move the money alone.

Card acceptance is not guaranteed, so declines fall through a three-tier ladder, all against the same held money: the card itself; failing that, a platform-initiated M-Pesa till payment drawn from the loaded balance; failing that, the requester pays the till directly and escrow reimburses them at close. The third tier posts to a requester_settled account rather than card float — money that never touched the card must never be counted as card spend, or the provenance claim collapses.

Every issuer and Daraja call carries an idempotency key derived from errand_id + kind + attempt, and callbacks are treated as unreliable: a reconciliation job replays payments, card loads and issuer authorisations every 15 minutes and is the only path that can mark money settled. Card float is a real ledger account — an authorisation that clears after a void is a known race, and the reconciler resolves it against escrow, never against the runner.

6. Identity, tiers and entitlements

KYC is gated: tier 0 can browse open errands and read profiles, and can do nothing else. tier 1 (name, DOB, National ID number, M-Pesa number, ID photos, liveness) may post and bid, capped at KSh 5,000 per errand. tier 2 adds proof of address and a payout-name match and lifts the cap to KSh 20,000. tier 3 is the runner tier and asks for what a requester does not have to give: a DCI certificate of good conduct under twelve months old, a next-of-kin contact never shown to requesters, and transport details where a boda or matatu is involved. Tier 3 plus a rating and completion rate above threshold is also what unlocks batching and direct invites.

Entitlements are cached as short-lived signed claims in the session token, so the gateway rejects an unentitled write without a round trip; the cache TTL is 5 minutes so a trust-and-safety suspension takes effect quickly. Cases re-check annually, and immediately on a name change, device change plus payout change, or a second upheld dispute.

7. Safety, escrow and adjudication

SOS opens a case, snapshots the live location and both verified identities, and dials 999 from the device. Handover is a QR scan rather than a spoken code: the requester's device displays a single-use token, the runner scans it, and the server records the scan with its geo-stamp — proof that cannot be guessed, overheard or claimed after the fact, and the settlement trigger for requester_confirm errands. Reported location is only shared for the duration of an active errand, plus 24 hours.

Escrow is the platform's answer to disputes, and it is deliberately symmetrical. A report filed before settlement freezes the escrow balance and voids the card: the requester cannot lose money that has not moved, and the runner cannot be stiffed for a purchase already evidenced. Neither side is asked to negotiate with the other, and neither side controls the outcome.

Judgement is passed by Legal Operations, a platform function with the only authority to split or reverse a frozen escrow. Trust & safety assembles an evidence pack from records the errand already produced — item photos with their vision scores, card authorisations, receipt OCR, the handover PIN and its geo-stamp, the message thread and both KYC tiers — and Legal Operations rules on it: release, split (in basis points), refund or void. Rulings are append-only, cite the evidence they rest on, target 24 hours, and bind both parties; each side may request one review, decided by a different reviewer. Adverse findings feed back into the matching penalties and, at the extreme, the KYC entitlement tier.

9. Open risks

  • Thin supply at launch. A sealed auction with no bidders is worse than auto-dispatch. Mitigation: fall back to direct offer at the guide ceiling to the top three runners after the first empty window.
  • Card acceptance at informal stalls. A market trader with no card terminal cannot be paid by card at all. The pilot needs the card to sit behind an M-Pesa till payment initiated from the loaded balance; where even that is absent, a tranche must be payable as cash-out to the runner against the same photo proof — which weakens the evidence chain and needs a lower cap.
  • Photo proof is gameable. A photo shows an item, not its price or that it was bought. Per-item price bands, per-market baselines and outlier flags do most of the work; expect a period where legal ops rulings, not the model, are the real control.
  • Issuer dependency. Per-errand single-use cards at low ticket sizes need an issuer that tolerates high card churn and sub-minute issuance. If no local issuer will, the fallback is one reloadable runner card with per-errand spend windows — weaker isolation, same release logic.
  • Escrow holds tie up funds. Cap plus 15% headroom is money the requester cannot use for the errand's duration; tune against real basket variance, and shorten the window rather than shrinking the headroom.
