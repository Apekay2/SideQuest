Side Qwest · mobile parity

One product, two platforms

The brand is identical on both: same Organic palette, same Caprasimo and Figtree, same information in the same order, same words. What changes is the mechanics each platform's users already have in their hands — where the primary action lives, how a sheet is dismissed, how a destructive choice is confirmed, and what a press feels like.

The rule the team can apply without asking: brand is shared, chrome is native. A runner who switches phones should recognise the app instantly and never have to relearn their thumb.

What differs, and why
Primary action

iOS puts Post in the tab bar as a fifth tab. Android drops it to four tabs and promotes Post to a floating action button. Both keep Post one thumb-reach away; neither fights the platform's own idea of where a create action lives.

Active state

iOS tints the icon and label terracotta. Android adds the M3 pill indicator behind the icon. Same colour token doing the work in both, drawn the way each platform draws it.

Bar metrics

49pt bar over a 34pt home indicator on iOS; 80dp bar on Android. Labels are 10px and 12px respectively — platform minimums, not a brand decision.

Touch floor

44pt on iOS, 48dp on Android. The rows use the larger of the two on each, which is why the regulars list is a touch taller on Android.

2 · Stall approval

The money screen — the one place where the two builds stay deliberately identical

Everything above the button adapts freely. This screen does not. The photo, the itemised prices, the running total against the remaining cap, and the three actions sit in the same order, at the same sizes, with the same words on both platforms — because a requester approving a spend should never have to re-read a layout, and because a support call about a wrong approval has to be answerable without asking which phone they hold.

Held identical on purpose
  • Order of information: photo, items, total, cap, actions.
  • The amount appears in the button label, not only in the total.
  • Approve is never optimistic on either platform — it enters a loading state and the sheet only closes when the server confirms.
  • Over cap: the total turns terracotta, Approve disables, and an inline sentence says by how much. No silent disabled button anywhere.
What still adapts
Dismissal

44×5 grabber and swipe-down on iOS; 32×4 drag handle plus the system back gesture on Android. A sheet that ignores Android's back button feels broken to half your users.

Feedback

Approve fires a light impact on iOS and a keyboard-tap effect on Android; success is a notification success and a single tick respectively.

Press state

iOS dims to the lighter terracotta on press. Android draws a ripple from the touch point. Same two tokens, different physics.

Same decision, native shape

Both offer exactly three ways out — decline, substitute, back off — and both say the two things that matter: the stall returns to the runner, and no money moves. Beyond that they diverge completely, and should.

iOS

Action sheet from the bottom, within thumb reach. Destructive option weighted in accentDeep rather than the system red — our palette carries the warning, and it still passes contrast at 17px.

Android

Centred M3 dialog, question as the headline, actions bottom-right with the confirming action last — the order Android users' hands already expect.

Not negotiable on either

Nothing destructive is ever the default focused action, and no confirm is dismissible by tapping the scrim.

The whole rule, on one page

Everything in the left column is generated from one source and cannot drift. Everything in the right column is resolved per platform at build time, so neither build carries the other's assumptions.

Shared · one source
Identical on both
■Colour, type, radii and spacing tokens
■Every string, through useT()
■Screen inventory and navigation graph
■Information order within every screen
■The money path — approval, ladder, settlement
■Error and empty copy, and what each offers next
■Never colour alone: icon plus label on every state
Adapted · resolved per platform
Native on each
▲Primary create action: fifth tab or FAB
▲Back: edge swipe and chevron, or system back and arrow
▲Confirms: action sheet, or M3 dialog
▲Press feedback: opacity dim, or ripple
▲Haptics, date and time pickers, share sheet
▲Touch floor: 44pt or 48dp
▲Permission priming, notification channels, deep links
Accessibility floor · both
Whichever is stricter
●Text scales to 200% without clipping — the approval sheet scrolls rather than truncates
●Every control has a label read by VoiceOver and TalkBack, including the amount on Approve
●Body text at 4.5:1 against its own ground, checked on the sunk and tinted surfaces too
●Reduce-motion honoured: the sheet fades instead of rising
●Swahili and English both fit every button at the largest supported size
